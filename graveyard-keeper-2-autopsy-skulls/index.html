Graveyard Keeper 2 Autopsy Guide: Skull Triage (Bury / Resurrect / Cremate)

This Graveyard Keeper 2 page answers one job: what do I do with this corpse? Use the triage tree, then jump to Zombies when you are preparing workers. For a full organ encyclopedia, use Wikily.

GK1 warning: Search results still surface Graveyard Keeper 1 Fandom organ tips. Those are a different game. Don’t apply GK1 skull math to GK2 decisions.

TL;DR — Triage in 30 seconds

Outcome Use when Next
Bury Body has useful white skulls and you can cover its red rows with grave decorations / wreaths so white rows can score Decorate the plot; improve headstone/fence if white rows are uncovered
Resurrect → zombie You need workers and the body is a labor candidate (more red skulls = more perk slots) Prep on this page → finish on Zombies
Cremate / dispose Body would wreck the yard and is not worth worker prep Crematorium when built (still earns a Burial Certificate); river dispose is a last resort with no certificate before that

White skull vs red skull (GK2 purpose split)

Treat this as a purpose split, then read the panel on the corpse you are holding:

Skull type Primary job Practical rule
White skulls Graveyard / burial quality A white skull row counts toward grave quality once matched by decoration / wreath quality on that grave
Red skulls Zombie worker perk slots Each red skull unlocks a zombie perk slot after resurrection — and for burial, each red row consumes decoration capacity without giving a positive quality point
Both matter Same corpse can lean yard or labor Decide the goal before you cut

Done when: You can say out loud whether today’s corpse is for yard or worker before you operate.

Public GK2 references (Wikily + Fextralife Corpse Quality) agree on this purpose split. Per-organ ±skull values vary by organ version — read the body in front of you; do not memorize a universal “always remove X” list.

Body baseline: Even after you strip removable parts, a corpse can keep residual white/red skulls (emptied bodies are not always skull-zero). Use that leftover lean to triage bury vs zombie vs burn.

Decision tree: What do I do with this corpse?

  1. Is the Resurrection Table / zombie pipeline online?
    - No → Prefer bury “good enough” bodies; cremate/dispose truly bad ones; don’t stockpile forever without a plan.
    - Yes → Continue.
  2. Is your goal right now graveyard quality or labor?
    - Yard quality → Keep / improve white-facing outcomes; remove red-facing organs when a clean extraction helps covering → Bury.
    - Labor → Prefer higher red-skull bodies with vital organs intact → Zombies hub.
  3. Is this corpse actively bad for the yard if buried as-is?
    - Yes, and not a worker candidate → Cremate after autopsy (Burial/death certificate still OK) — do not force-bury yard-toxic bodies. River dispose = faster but no certificate (last resort before Crematorium).
    - Yes, but salvageable with autopsy → Autopsy workflow below, then read the panel again.
    - No → Bury or resurrect per goal in step 2.
  4. Are you tempted to use GK1 organ memory?
    - Stop. Read the GK2 corpse panel or open Wikily GK2 corpses — never trust GK1 Fandom by default.

Autopsy workflow (steps)

GK2 rule (not GK1): Inspect white/red + part variants before cutting. Same organ type ≠ fixed skulls — always read this corpse panel.

Step What you do Done when
1 Place the corpse on the Autopsy Table Station accepts the body
2 Inspect the white/red skull readout and each part's color/star variants before cutting You know starting state on this body — not a memorized GK1 organ list
3 Remove / treat organs with a goal (yard vs worker) Each action has a reason — not "harvest everything" (fat+blood is not a universal red→white recipe)
4 Read the skull count again after each meaningful change You stop when the triage outcome is clear
5 Choose bury / resurrect / cremate-dispose Body leaves the table with a decision recorded

Study each new organ at the Study Table before you store it; see how to get tech points.

Extraction outcomes (conceptual): Operations run on ticks tied to Anatomy mastery. Enough successes keep the organ as an item; mid results remove it ruined; too few successes can leave a Surgeon's Error (worse skull balance). Exact tick tables → Wikily Reference.

Early vs later: Early — practice reading skulls and avoiding obviously bad burials. Later — deliberate worker prep once collars and perk slots matter. Organ-by-organ encyclopedia → Wikily, not here.

Preparing bodies for zombies

Once resurrection is available:

  1. Pick corpses for labor, not every body.
  2. Prefer more red / fewer white for workers; white skulls do nothing for a zombie — they matter only for graves. Keep all six vital organs (skull, brain, heart, guts, bones, skin): a corpse missing one cannot be resurrected. Soft parts like Blood/Flesh/Fat are a different category.
  3. Match collar tier to the red-skull count you are aiming for (table below).
  4. Hand off to the zombies pipeline for Resurrection Table, assignment, collars. → Graveyard Keeper 2 Zombies
  5. Mid-game automation still cares about worker quality. → Automation · Red Skull Zombies
Collar Red skulls Prep context
Bronze Zombie Collar 0–5 First mass-producible worker target ≈ 5 red
Golden Zombie Collar 0–10 Next step once gold collars unlock
Steel Zombie Collar 0–99 Late / min-max — full crafting on the Red Skull page

Each red skull becomes a perk slot; see zombie mastery & perks.

Over the cap is refused: the Resurrection Table rejects a corpse with more reds than its collar allows, and a body under the cap wastes slots. Free surgery part-swaps (stamina/sanity) are not the same as embalming-only slots that need fluids.

Rule of thumb: aim for ≈ 5 red on your first workers and 10 once gold collars unlock. Deep 5 / 10 / 36-red part-swap sequences, Steel min-max, and full crafting steps are on Red Skull Zombies. This guide covers prep and collar matching only.

Wreaths / covers / decorations rule

Agreed conceptual rule across Wikily and Fextralife Corpse Quality:

  • Each red skull row must be covered by decoration / wreath capacity before white rows start contributing positive graveyard quality points.
  • A red-covered row still does not score a positive quality point — it only spends covering capacity.
  • Once all useful white rows are covered, extra decoration on that corpse cannot invent more white skulls.

Every headstone, fence and decoration value is listed on Graveyard quality.

FAQ

Q: When should I bury vs resurrect vs cremate/dispose?
A: Bury for yard goals when white rows can be covered; resurrect when you need workers and the body is a red-skull candidate; after autopsy, if still yard-toxic and not a worker candidate → cremate (certificate OK) rather than force-bury. River dispose = no certificate last resort. Use the decision tree above.

Q: What are white skulls for vs red skulls for in Graveyard Keeper 2?
A: White leans graveyard/burial quality (after decoration covers red rows). Red leans zombie perk slots and consumes covering capacity on graves. Do not treat GK1 Fandom numbers as GK2 facts.

Q: What makes a “bad corpse” for the graveyard?
A: A body whose red rows eat decoration capacity so white rows never score — or a body you bury without enough headstone/fence quality. When in doubt, autopsy to diagnose, then bury / cremate / hold for zombies.

Q: Which autopsy actions are safe early vs save for later?
A: Early: learn to read skulls and avoid obviously harmful burials. Later: deliberate worker prep and embalming once resurrection and collars matter. Don’t delay first workers for perfect reds.

Q: How does this page connect to zombie workers?
A: This page decides which bodies become workers; Zombies covers unlock, Resurrection Table, assignment, and optimize stages. Deep red-skull optimization → Red Skull Zombies.

Q: Why do other guides disagree / why might GK1 advice be wrong?
A: Patches change the numbers, and GK1 advice gets mixed in. Go by the GK2 corpse panel on the body you hold; when public GK2 guides conflict on organ math, prefer Wikily's GK2 data over a compromise table.

Q: Do embalming solutions belong here?
A: Conceptually: corpse vs zombie solutions shift white/red without surgery. Exact mix recipes → Wikily embalming table.
